# Pilot's Guide

How to fly the Country Airfield Flight Simulator: controls, views, settings, and procedures for
each aircraft. All speeds are indicated airspeeds in knots. The airfield is 50 ft above sea
level, with a 600 m gravel runway (18/36) and a parallel grass runway 30 m to the east.

## Getting started

1. Open the sim (see the README). The Air-Bike starts lined up on gravel runway 36, engine idling.
2. Press **Esc** for the menu: pick the aircraft, the start position (the apron or a runway
   threshold), wind, time of day, controls, units, graphics and sound. Settings are saved in
   your browser.
3. Press any key or click to start the sound.
4. Add power and fly. Press **H** to hide the key help along the bottom, **I** to hide the HUD.

## Controls

### Keyboard (on by default; **K** toggles)

| Key | Control |
|---|---|
| ↑ / ↓ | Stick forward (nose down) / back (nose up). Released, it centres |
| ← / → | Stick left / right (roll) |
| Z / C | Left / right rudder (and nosewheel or tailwheel steering) |
| + / − or PgUp / PgDn | Throttle up / down |
| Home / End | Propeller rpm lever up / down (Decathlon constant-speed propeller) |
| B | Brakes (shared between the wheels with the rudder: steer with Z/C while braking) |
| Q | Prerotator (gyro, hold): spins the rotor up before take-off |
| E | Engine start / stop |
| U | Auto-coordinated rudder (keeps the slip ball centred for you) |

### Joystick and pedals

The **Logitech Extreme 3D Pro** works straight away:
- **Stick:** pitch and roll.
- **Twist grip:** rudder.
- **Slider:** throttle.
- **Trigger:** brakes.
- **Thumb button:** gyro prerotator.
- **Hat switch:** look around in the cockpit.

For any other joystick or pedals, press **O** (or Esc, then *Control mapping…*) to bind and
calibrate axes and buttons. A connected joystick's throttle overrides the keyboard throttle.

**J** turns the mouse into the stick (move it from the centre of the screen).

### Wind

The menu sets everything; these keys adjust it while flying.

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| N | Next wind preset |
| [ / ] | Wind speed −/+ 1 kt |
| , / . | Wind direction −/+ 10°. From 090 or 270 is a direct crosswind on runways 36 and 18 |
| ; / ' | Gusts −/+ 1 kt (the strongest gust above the mean wind) |
| / | Turbulence: none → light → moderate → severe |

The windsock on the tower roof shows the wind at 10 m. It fills one band per 3 kt and is fully out at 15 kt.

## Instruments

**HUD** (top left, **I** to hide):
- Airspeed (always knots), altitude and height above ground, heading, vertical speed.
- G with its highest and lowest values, wind.
- Engine rpm, throttle, fuel, cylinder head (CHT) and exhaust (EGT) temperatures.
- Angle of attack and slip ball. Manifold pressure and propeller rpm setting (Decathlon), rotor rpm (gyro).

Warnings turn the HUD red and stay on screen even with the HUD hidden:
- STALL; G LIMIT, OVER-STRESS; VNE.
- Gyro: LOW G, PUSHOVER, LOW ROTOR RPM, ROTOR OVERSPEED, ROTOR TEETER.
- FUEL STARVED, LOW OIL PRESSURE.

**Panel** (cockpit view): flight instruments across the top, the slip ball below the airspeed
indicator, the heading instrument to its left and the engine gauges to its right.
- **Air-Bike and gyro:** a wet compass (it reads "backwards", like the real one).
- **Decathlon:** an attitude indicator, a directional gyro and a G-meter with max/min needles.
- **Gyro:** rotor rpm on a digital display, which turns red outside 200–400 rpm.

The HUD units (feet or metres, ft/min or m/s, °C or °F, litres or US gallons, inHg or hPa) are
set in the menu. Airspeed is always knots.

## General technique

- **Slip ball:** keep it centred with rudder ("step on the ball"), or turn on auto-rudder (U).
- **Taildraggers (Air-Bike, Decathlon):** hold the stick back on the ground to keep the tail
  down. Steer with rudder (the tailwheel follows it). Brake only with the tail firmly down.
- **Crosswind:** on the take-off roll, hold the stick into wind and keep straight with rudder.
  On approach, crab into wind. From short final, lower the into-wind wing and hold the
  runway line with rudder (the wing-low method), so you touch down without drifting.
- **Gusts:** add half the gust value to your approach speed.

## Air-Bike 103

An open-cockpit ultralight with a single-cylinder Hirth two-stroke and a belt reduction drive.
It is light and draggy: it slows quickly when you reduce power. **No brakes.**

| Speed | kt |
|---|---|
| Stall (power off) | 24 |
| Approach | 33 |
| Best climb (about 475 fpm) | 39 |
| Cruise (75 % power, about 6,000 rpm) | 47 |
| Never exceed (Vne) | 69 |

Engine: 6,500 rpm maximum. Watch the CHT (red line 250 °C) and the EGT (red line 650 °C) in a long climb.

**Take-off** (about 76 m):
1. Line up with the stick back and smoothly add full power.
2. Keep straight with rudder, and hold the fuselage about 10° nose-up (three-point attitude).
3. It flies off by itself at about 30 kt.
4. Lower the nose to climb at 39 kt.

**Cruise:** about 75 % throttle gives level flight at 47 kt with the fuselage level. Full power
climbs about 10° nose-up; idle descends about 10° nose-down.

**Approach and landing:**
1. Fly a 500 ft circuit and a gentle final at 33 kt, using power to hold the glide path.
2. At about 2–3 m keep a little power on (it bleeds speed fast) and ease the stick back.
3. Touch down three-point (nose about 10° up) at about 26 kt.
4. Close the throttle, stick fully back.

The roll-out is long with no brakes: about 110 m on grass, 160 m on gravel. Plan to land near
the threshold.

**Stall:** gentle. Release back pressure and add power. Keep the wings level with rudder rather than aileron.

## Super Decathlon 8KCAB

A two-seat aerobatic taildragger (flown solo from the front seat), with a 180 hp fuel-injected
Lycoming with inverted fuel and oil systems, and a constant-speed propeller (Home/End sets its
rpm, 2,000–2,700). Toe brakes.

| Speed | kt |
|---|---|
| Stall | 49 |
| Best angle of climb (Vx) | 50 |
| Approach | 60 |
| Best rate of climb (Vy, about 1,130 fpm) | 71 |
| Manoeuvring (Va): full control deflection only below this | 93 |
| Normal operating limit (Vno, top of the green arc) | 139 |
| Never exceed (Vne, red line) | 173 |
| Cruise (75 % power at 7,500 ft) | 122 TAS |

Limits: +6 g / −5 g (the G-meter keeps your highest and lowest), 2,700 rpm. A stall warning
horn sounds 3° of angle of attack before the stall.

**Take-off** (about 177 m):
1. Propeller lever fully up (2,700 rpm), stick back.
2. Smoothly add full power, keeping straight with rudder (it swings left with power).
3. Hold the three-point attitude (about 11° nose-up). It lifts off at about 57 kt.
4. Climb at 71 kt (Vy), or 50 kt (Vx) to clear obstacles.

**Cruise:** set power with the throttle (manifold pressure) and rpm with the propeller lever.
75 % power gives about 122 kt true airspeed at 7,500 ft.

**Approach and landing** (roll about 152 m with braking):
1. Circuit at 90 kt.
2. Final at 60 kt. The idling constant-speed propeller is a strong air brake, so keep a little power on.
3. From about 5 m, flare to the three-point attitude and hold off until it settles.
4. Stick fully back, then brake (B) once the tail is down.

**Aerobatics:**
- Stay within +6/−5 g and below Va (93 kt) when using full control deflection.
- The inverted fuel and oil systems keep the engine running upside down.

**Spin recovery:**
1. Throttle idle.
2. Ailerons neutral.
3. Full rudder against the spin.
4. Stick forward until the rotation stops.
5. Centre the rudder and ease out of the dive.

**Stall:** clean, with the horn first. A wing drops if you are out of balance. Recover with the
stick forward, full power, then level the wings.

## Bensen B-8M gyroplane

A single-seat gyroplane with a McCulloch flat-4 two-stroke driving a pusher propeller, and a
free-spinning (autorotating) two-blade rotor. The rotor is not driven in flight: air flowing up
through it keeps it turning. The stick tilts the rotor head. The gyro here has a modern
layout, with the propeller thrust line within 2 in of the centre of gravity (see
docs/STATUS.md), so full power does not pitch the nose down in a pushover. Brakes on all
three wheels.

| Speed | kt |
|---|---|
| Best climb (about 1,100 fpm) | 45 |
| Approach (power off) | 45 |
| Cruise (45 % power, about 3,450 rpm) | 53–56 |
| Maximum / never exceed | 82 |

A gyro does not stall like a wing: slow down too much and it simply sinks faster. Rotor rpm
limits: 200 minimum in flight, 400 maximum.

**Prerotation and take-off** (about 30 m):
1. Hold the brakes (B).
2. Engage the prerotator (hold Q or the thumb button) and set about 2,600 engine rpm. At idle
   the clutch only slips (the HUD says "add power: ~2600 engine rpm").
3. Hold until the rotor reaches about 300–330 rpm (20–25 s).
4. Release the prerotator and the brakes, then smoothly add full power.
5. With the stick back, the rotor loads up and the nosewheel lifts at about 13–15 kt.
6. **Then ease the stick forward** to hold the nose about 10–15° up, balanced on the main wheels.
7. It lifts off at about 25 kt. Climb at 45 kt.

**Do not hold full back stick after the nosewheel lifts.** In a gyro the stick commands pitch
rate: the nose keeps rising, the rotor becomes an air brake, the speed falls, and the gyro rolls
over on its side.

**Crosswind:** hold the stick into wind during prerotation, the take-off roll and the landing
roll, so the wind cannot tilt the spinning rotor and push the gyro sideways.

**Cruise:** hands-off, the gyro flies itself. At 45 % power it holds about 53 kt, level and
straight. More power climbs, less descends; the stick sets the speed. Let go of the stick in a
turn and it eases back to wings-level in a few seconds.

**Approach and landing** (roll about 25–30 m):
1. Power to idle, stick for 45 kt. It descends at about 800 fpm in a steady autorotation.
2. At about 8 m above the ground, ease the stick back to reduce the sink and bleed the speed off.
3. Touch down on the main wheels at low speed.
4. Stick fully back (the rotor is a powerful brake), then brakes.

**Hazards and warnings:**
- **LOW G / PUSHOVER:** pushing the stick sharply forward unloads the rotor. Rotor rpm decays
  and control is lost at zero g. Never push over abruptly. If it happens: power off, ease the
  stick back to reload the rotor.
- **LOW ROTOR RPM** (below 200): reload the rotor, with a gentle back stick and positive g.
- **ROTOR OVERSPEED** (above 400): reduce the load. Avoid sharp pull-ups at high speed.
- **ROTOR TEETER** (above 9°): large, abrupt stick movements make the rotor see-saw towards its
  stops, where a blade can strike the propeller. Ease off the controls.
